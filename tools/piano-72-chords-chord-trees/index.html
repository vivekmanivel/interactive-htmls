72 Piano Chords

Every major & minor chord, in every inversion – practice sheet using the “chord trees” method (credits at the end).

1. Where "72" comes from

12+12=24×3=72
  • 12 major chords (one for each note)
  • 12 minor chords (one for each note)
  • 24 chords in total
  • Each can be played 3 ways (root position, 1st inversion, 2nd inversion) → 72 shapes

2. What everything means

Triad

A 3-note chord: Root, 3rd, 5th. Every chord on this page is a triad.

Root

The note the chord is named after. In C major the root is C.

MAJOR

Root + 4 semitones (major 3rd) + 3 more (minor 3rd on top). Sounds bright, happy, open. C major = C E G

MINOR

Root + 3 semitones (minor 3rd) + 4 more. Sounds darker, sadder, more emotional. Written with a small "m". C minor = C E♭ G

Semitone

The smallest step on the piano – one key to the very next key (white or black).

Inversion

Same three notes, different order from bottom to top. Same chord, different shape.

Root position

Root at the bottom, 5th on top: R – 3 – 5.

1st inversion

3rd at the bottom, root on top: 3 – 5 – R.

2nd inversion

5th at the bottom, 3rd on top: 5 – R – 3.

Sharps / flats

♯ = one key up, ♭ = one key down. Same black key, two names: A♭ = G♯, D♭ = C♯. The name depends on the chord.

3. The method: chord trees

Don't memorise 72 shapes one by one. Pick one note and keep it on top of your right hand. It never moves. Only the chord underneath changes.

  • Exactly 6 triads contain any given note: 3 major + 3 minor (it can be the root, the 3rd or the 5th).
  • That note is the common note – it links all 6 chords, so they flow together smoothly.
  • Play the root of each chord in your left hand, the triad in your right.
  • Pick a different top note and you get a new tree: 12 trees × 6 chords = 72.
  • 6 trees = 36 chords = halfway.

Examples: the D tree is D → B♭ → G → Bm → Gm → Dm. The A♭ tree is A♭, G♯m, D♭, C♯m, E, Fm – A♭ never moves.

4. Know your keys

  • C = the white key just left of the group of two black keys.
  • G = the white key between the 1st and 2nd black keys of the group of three.
  • Use C and G as landmarks, then count up or down from them. Black keys have two names (e.g. C♯ = D♭).

The keyboards in the chord cards run from C to B over three octaves. C and G are labelled on every one.

7. How to practise

  1. Take one tree. Find the top note and hold it as your right-hand top finger.
  2. Play the 6 chords in order, root in the left hand. Go slowly – smooth beats fast.
  3. Repeat until you can do it without looking, then go backwards.
  4. Say the chord name out loud and whether it's major or minor.
  5. Move to the next tree. Revisit finished trees to keep them fresh.
